# Language Tracker app icon — concept directions

- Date: 2026-09-05
- Status: Stage 1 complete; awaiting product-owner direction
- Scope: Concept definition only. No artwork or generated imagery was produced.

## Product and visual context

Language Tracker is built around two parallel records: exact study time and
newly learned vocabulary. Its most characteristic visual device is the yearly
heatmap, where repeated small actions accumulate into a legible history. The
interface is calm, spacious, geometric, and largely flat. Primary blue identifies
Study Time, emerald identifies Vocabulary, and warm yellow-to-green states show
positive study accumulation. Rounded cards and compact square heatmap cells
provide the recurring geometry.

The current icon combines an open book with a green dot on a blue rounded
square. It communicates education, but its isolated dot does not strongly convey
tracking, history, regularity, or levels. The directions below retain the
product's clarity and color language while seeking a more ownable central mark.

## Direction 1 — Progress Mosaic

1. **Core symbol and meaning.** A compact field of seven rounded tiles changes
   from quiet blue to green and resolves into a single upward/rightward path.
   The tiles represent days; the coherent path represents progress created by
   consistency rather than one dramatic achievement.
2. **Connection to Language Tracker.** This direction comes directly from the
   product's Study Time and Vocabulary heatmaps, while the path introduces the
   idea of advancing through a language over time.
3. **Geometry and composition.** Use a centered 3-by-3 implied grid with only
   seven large rounded squares. Two empty positions create a strong diagonal
   channel in negative space. The terminal tile is slightly larger or joined to
   its neighbor, creating a clear destination without an arrowhead. Avoid a
   literal miniature heatmap.
4. **Proposed palette.** Deep blue background `#155DFC`; tiles in off-white
   `#F8FAFC`, warm yellow `#F7C95E`, sage `#78B76D`, and forest `#15803D`.
   A reduced two-color blue/off-white treatment should also work.
5. **At 32–48 px.** Seven separated masses remain readable if gaps are at least
   roughly one device pixel at 32 px. Yellow and sage can merge into one lighter
   progress color at favicon size, leaving the diagonal rhythm and terminal tile
   as the identifier.
6. **Mask adaptation.** The active grid occupies approximately the central 56%
   of the canvas. A full-bleed blue field supports circle, square, squircle, and
   rounded-square crops. No tile or essential gap enters the outer 20% ring.
7. **Strengths.** Highly specific to the actual product; compact; calm; easy to
   reconstruct as clean geometry; visually related to both trackers; distinctive
   without letters or flags.
8. **Possible drawbacks.** A plain grid could resemble a generic analytics or
   habit app. The negative-space path must be intentionally shaped so the result
   feels like one emblem, not a dashboard thumbnail.
9. **Production PWA suitability.** **9/10.** It has the best combination of
   product truth, small-size resilience, mask safety, and a credible unique
   silhouette.

## Direction 2 — Dialogue Dial

1. **Core symbol and meaning.** Two broad interlocking arcs form both a speech
   exchange and a clock dial. One short radial notch suggests measured time;
   the central negative space suggests an ongoing conversation.
2. **Connection to Language Tracker.** Speech represents language practice, and
   the dial represents exact minutes, repeated pace windows, and regular study.
   The paired arcs can also stand for Study Time and Vocabulary as parallel
   signals.
3. **Geometry and composition.** Build a nearly circular emblem from two thick
   C-shaped bands facing one another. Use one deliberate opening at lower left
   and one at upper right; square or softly rounded band ends can imply speech
   tails without drawing conventional chat bubbles. Add at most one substantial
   clock notch—no hands, numerals, or fine ticks.
4. **Proposed palette.** Dark navy background `#0B1220`; primary blue arc
   `#60A5FA`; emerald arc `#34D399`; off-white notch `#F8FAFC`. A light-home-screen
   alternative uses primary blue `#155DFC` behind the same bright arcs.
5. **At 32–48 px.** The two thick arcs read first as a bold circular mark. The
   clock notch remains visible; the speech interpretation becomes a rewarding
   secondary read rather than a detail required for recognition.
6. **Mask adaptation.** A circular core is naturally compatible with round
   masks and remains centered inside square and squircle masks. The mark should
   stay within 58–62% of the canvas width, with the background extending fully
   to every edge.
7. **Strengths.** Combines language and time without a translator glyph; strong
   dark-mode presence; simple enough for precise vector construction; balanced
   and app-like.
8. **Possible drawbacks.** Circular speech and clock combinations are common.
   If the arc endings become obvious bubble tails or the notch resembles a play
   button, the mark may feel generic or media-oriented.
9. **Production PWA suitability.** **8/10.** Very legible and mask-safe, but it
   needs careful negative-space design to become ownable.

## Direction 3 — Level Trail

1. **Core symbol and meaning.** One continuous folded trail rises through three
   broad terraces and ends in a small open portal. It represents moving through
   levels while preserving the path already travelled.
2. **Connection to Language Tracker.** The terraces reference A0–C2 progression,
   the continuous trail references historical records, and its repeated rise
   reflects streaks and cumulative practice without implying automatic
   promotion.
3. **Geometry and composition.** Use a single heavy ribbon with three horizontal
   segments connected by two vertical turns, simplified into a compact zigzag.
   The ribbon and its negative-space counterpart should form a stable diamond
   or shield-like silhouette. The end portal is a substantial rounded opening,
   not a tiny dot or trophy.
4. **Proposed palette.** Primary blue field `#155DFC`; trail in off-white
   `#F8FAFC`; final segment or portal in emerald `#10B981`; optional restrained
   warm-yellow hinge `#F7C95E` only at larger sizes.
5. **At 32–48 px.** The stepped diagonal survives as one bold stroke. The portal
   becomes a simple green cap or negative-space opening; the optional yellow
   hinge is omitted below 48 px.
6. **Mask adaptation.** The diamond-like core stays inside a central 60% square.
   Its diagonal energy prevents a circular mask from feeling static, while a
   full-bleed field handles squircle and rounded-square masks without separate
   compositions.
7. **Strengths.** Communicates long-term progression and history more clearly
   than an education cliché; creates a memorable single silhouette; works in
   one color if needed.
8. **Possible drawbacks.** A stepped trail can drift toward growth-chart,
   logistics, or game-level imagery. The endpoint must not imply that the app
   awards or automatically advances CEFR levels.
9. **Production PWA suitability.** **8.5/10.** Strong and scalable, with slightly
   less immediate language meaning than the other directions.

## Direction 4 — Tracked L

1. **Core symbol and meaning.** An abstract lowercase `l` is constructed from a
   vertical measure and a circular tracking orbit. The orbit's gap and a single
   moving marker express an ongoing record rather than a completed badge.
2. **Connection to Language Tracker.** The `l` quietly anchors the product name
   and the language domain; the orbit represents time windows, repetition, and
   accumulated history. The vertical measure can suggest both a timeline and
   progress toward the next level.
3. **Geometry and composition.** Use one thick vertical rounded bar offset left
   of center. A broad, incomplete ring wraps its lower half and crosses behind
   it, creating the `l` through negative space rather than typography. One large
   square marker on the ring echoes a heatmap cell. Do not add a second letter
   or a literal clock hand.
4. **Proposed palette.** Off-white background `#F8FAFC`; ink mark `#0F172A`;
   primary-blue orbit segment `#155DFC`; emerald marker `#10B981`. For required
   opaque dark presentation, use dark navy `#0B1220`, off-white mark, and the
   same blue/emerald accents.
5. **At 32–48 px.** It reads as a bold abstract hook/ring first and as an `l`
   second. The square marker must be large enough to remain intentional, while
   the orbit gap stays wider than a thin decorative cut.
6. **Mask adaptation.** The orbit is centered within the central safe circle;
   the vertical bar stops well short of the top crop boundary. Separate light
   and dark full-bleed background exports can preserve contrast, while the core
   geometry remains identical for every mask.
7. **Strengths.** Most brand-oriented and least illustrative; compact; suitable
   for a future wordmark system; recognizable in monochrome; avoids books,
   flags, and translation symbols.
8. **Possible drawbacks.** The `l` can be mistaken for a generic location pin,
   power symbol, or analytics logo. It communicates education less directly and
   would require the most refinement to prove distinctiveness.
9. **Production PWA suitability.** **7.5/10.** Potentially the most ownable after
   refinement, but currently the least self-explanatory.

## Recommendation

Proceed with **Progress Mosaic** for Stage 2. It turns the product's most
recognizable interaction—the accumulation of days in two heatmaps—into a simple
mark, while the negative-space route adds progress without relying on a generic
arrow, book, globe, flag, or translation bubble. It also gives the strongest
foundation for the required maskable icon: the field can be full bleed and the
entire semantic mark can stay comfortably inside the safe circle.

For visual exploration, the most important constraint is to avoid drawing a
miniature calendar. The seven tiles should lock into one memorable silhouette,
and the implied path should remain recognizable in a two-color 32 px reduction.
Stage 2 should compare variants that change the tile arrangement, the treatment
of the terminal tile, and the negative-space path—not merely the palette.

No direction is selected by this document. Visual development must wait for an
explicit product-owner choice or instruction to combine named elements.
